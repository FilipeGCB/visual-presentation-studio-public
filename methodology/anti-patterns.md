# Presentation Anti-Patterns

Reject patterns that simulate sophistication without increasing comprehension.

- Excessive rounded cards flatten hierarchy and make every idea look equivalent.
- Purposeless glassmorphism reduces legibility and signals template-first design.
- Arbitrary gradients and decorative neon add visual noise without meaning.
- Repeated three-card grids substitute a component pattern for narrative architecture.
- Generic icons and corporate emoji weaken specificity and credibility.
- Dashboardization turns any number into a control panel even when the story needs an argument.
- Motion on everything dilutes focus and harms accessibility.
- Generic titles hide the actual claim.
- Dense text shrunk to fit preserves pixels while destroying meaning.
- Stock or generated imagery without narrative function decorates instead of communicating.
- Asset patchwork combines individually competent images with unrelated lighting, framing, spatial logic, or narrative roles and makes the result feel assembled after the layout.
- Approval laundering calls a study, candidate, or direction “approved” even though the user never approved that exact asset or build.
- Screenshot-only QA judges an interaction from a transient or static frame without exercising and recording its real state.

Use the governing question: “Does this look designed specifically for this story, or like a generic interface produced by AI?”
