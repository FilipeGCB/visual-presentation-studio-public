# Visual Research

Visual research serves a defined communication problem. It is not a tour of fashionable interfaces.

## Research brief

Record the thesis, audience, experience mode, desired emotion, information types, identity constraints, required assets, technical route, and questions the research must answer.

## When live research is required

Complete live reference research before Gate 1 when:

- the user names a site, gallery, component source, or reference;
- the requested standard is premium, current, or best-in-class;
- the direction depends on motion, sticky behavior, parallax, scrollytelling, or another interaction whose value cannot be judged from a thumbnail.

Inspect 3–5 relevant references unless fewer defensible examples exist. Research may be omitted only when a supplied identity already fixes the direction and no reference-dependent behavior is proposed; record the reason. Exploring sources is autonomous and does not require a micro-approval per reference.

## Source classes

- 21st.dev and component libraries for interaction mechanics and implementable patterns;
- Awwwards and other curated experience galleries for composition, pacing, and spatial ideas;
- Dribbble and design portfolios for focused visual treatments, with extra scrutiny for production realism;
- editorial, exhibition, publishing, and reporting work for narrative hierarchy;
- motion libraries and examples for functional transitions;
- data visualization references for encoding and annotation;
- iconography, photography, illustration, maps, diagrams, screenshots, and AI-generated image studies when the narrative calls for them.

## Capture format

For each useful reference record:

- live URL, source, and creator when known;
- observed resting composition and scroll or interaction behavior;
- transferable principle;
- narrative problem it may solve;
- compatibility with the approved experience and stack;
- accessibility, performance, rights, and copying risks;
- what must be adapted rather than reproduced.
- adoption, adaptation, or rejection decision.

When behavior matters, inspect the rendered page rather than relying on screenshots or prose. Compare the implemented result with the approved reference matrix during Visual Inspection.

Discard references selected only because they look fashionable. Never copy identity, text, proprietary imagery, or a near-identical exclusive layout. Generated imagery is an asset-production method, not a substitute for reference research.
