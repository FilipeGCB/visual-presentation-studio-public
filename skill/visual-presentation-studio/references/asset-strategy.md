# Asset Strategy

Every asset must prove, explain, orient, compare, humanize, or create intentional narrative rhythm. Decoration alone is not a function.

## Choose deliberately

For each narrative need, decide among:

- photography for real people, places, products, atmosphere, or evidence;
- documentary imagery for traceable real-world context;
- conceptual imagery for an abstract thesis or metaphor;
- AI-generated imagery when a specific unavailable concept materially improves comprehension and synthetic origin is acceptable;
- editorial composition when type, image, and structure must work as one argument;
- illustration when abstraction, consistency, or impossible viewpoints help;
- diagram for relationships, sequence, hierarchy, systems, or causality;
- data visualization for quantitative comparison, trend, distribution, composition, or relationship;
- screenshot for product, process, evidence, or interface state;
- icon for rapid recognition inside an established system;
- no asset when typography and composition communicate more clearly.

Do not force imagery into every screen. Do not turn every dataset into a dashboard.

## Plan the system before generating

Do not generate or integrate a primary visual asset until the storyboard records its function and the asset brief defines:

- visual family and relationship to the approved direction;
- mother scene and derived states when a sequence is needed;
- camera, lighting, material, palette, and spatial logic;
- focal point, text-safe area, and desktop/mobile crop behavior;
- narrative state or scroll chapter served;
- negative constraints and clichés to avoid;
- approval scope and current status.

Primary assets should feel like one commissioned series. A group of unrelated images added after layout is asset patchwork even when each image is individually strong.

## Asset Board Checkpoint

When imagery materially defines identity or composition, present the visual family and primary candidates as a focused sub-decision inside Gate 1. This checkpoint approves series logic and named assets; it does not create a third human gate or require approval for every minor icon.

Use these states precisely:

`briefed → study → candidate → approved for production → applied → validated in composition → final`

Direction approval does not advance an individual asset. A study is not an approved photograph. `Applied` means placed in the build; it still requires validation in the rendered composition.

## Source and rights checks

- Prefer user-provided, licensed, public-domain, or clearly reusable sources.
- Treat screenshots and internal imagery as potentially sensitive.
- Use visual research for inspiration, not unlicensed copying.
- Record when AI generation is used and keep prompts aligned with the approved direction, asset brief, and series language.
- Never send confidential source material to an external generation service without authorization.

## Technical checks

Define crop behavior, focal point, resolution, format, compression, transparency, color treatment, alt text, responsive variants, and fallback. Validate the asset in the rendered composition and intended interaction states; an isolated image preview is insufficient. A static crop is not evidence of live mobile behavior.
